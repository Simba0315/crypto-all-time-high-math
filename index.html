How far crypto is from its all-time high, and the math of getting back

Adam Shanin · 25 November 2026

After a long fall, the question many people ask about a coin is "how far is it from its high?". The answer is easy to look up and easy to misread, because the distance down and the distance back up are not the same number. This page uses figures from the all-time high tracker on CryptoBubbles, read on 25 November 2026.

Where the market stood

On that day none of the 100 largest coins was within 10% of its all-time high (ATH), and the median coin in the top 100 was 87.6% below its record. The large coins:

The asymmetry

A fall of x% needs a rise of x / (100 - x) to undo it. A 50% fall needs +100%. A 75% fall needs +300%, a fourfold move. A 90% fall needs +900%. That is why Bitcoin, at -53.8%, needs to slightly more than double, while XRP, only about 20 points further down at -75.3%, needs to quadruple. The last few percent of a drawdown are the most expensive to recover.

Why the median coin is so far down

Most of the top 100 set their highs during a short burst of excitement, often in the year they launched. New tokens also tend to have small circulating supplies at first and large unlocks later, which push the price down as supply grows. So a median drawdown near -88% says less about today's market than about how most tokens are launched and priced.

What an ATH does not tell you

  • Whether it will be reached again. Some coins have never returned to their peak; Terra Luna Classic is still 100% below its 2022 high.
  • Whether the high was meaningful. A record set on thin volume, on one exchange, is a weak reference point.
  • Anything about value. A coin far below its high is not "cheap" by that fact alone.

New highs in a falling market

Even on a weak day, 23 coins in the top 1,200 had set a new ATH within the previous seven days. Most were tokenised funds and yield products priced close to $1, plus a few tokenised stocks. That is a reminder to look at what a coin is before reading anything into a record.

A better way to use the number

Instead of asking how far a coin is from its peak, compare drawdowns within a group. If Bitcoin is 54% below its high and a large coin you follow is 77% below, the second coin has fallen much further in the same market, and the reason is worth reading about. Distance from the high works as a measure of how much damage a cycle did, not as a target price.
